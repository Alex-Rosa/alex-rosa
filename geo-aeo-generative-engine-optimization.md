In this video from Silicon Valley Girl (The Real AI Opportunity | The Ultimate Guide to Become Visible in AI), Marina shares a step-by-step roadmap for Generative Engine Optimization (GEO / AEO) to make sure AI models (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude) find, understand, and recommend your content or brand.
---

### Step 1: Diagnose Your Visibility

* **Audit your website with an AI chatbot [[03:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=230)]:** Paste your site URL into Claude, ChatGPT, or another chatbot and ask: *"How visible are we in AI search, and what's stopping us from showing up?"* This generates a custom checklist of technical issues (such as missing HTML metadata or content hidden behind client-side JavaScript).

### Step 2: Build a Crawler-Readable Site

* **Serve pre-rendered/static HTML [[07:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=430)]:** AI bots (Perplexity, ChatGPT, Claude crawlers) do not execute heavy client-side JavaScript well. Ensure full content is available directly in the raw page source upon load without needing clicks or dynamic rendering.
* **Publish full transcripts in plain text [[07:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=454)]:** Place the entire episode transcript directly on each page (8,000–15,000 words per episode). This gives models direct source text to quote and associate with your name.
* **Dedicate a clean URL per episode/asset [[06:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=405)]:** Give every piece of content its own crawlable, dedicated URL containing summaries, guest bios, and transcripts.
* **Stack used [[06:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=366)]:** She noted using Claude Code to write the site, Vercel to host, and Railway to pull raw data and dynamically generate pages.

### Step 3: Standardize Entity Identity Across the Web

* **Update Wikidata entries [[08:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=504)]:** Search models rely heavily on structured knowledge graphs like Wikidata (which feeds Google's Knowledge Panel and Gemini). Make sure your `instance of` and `occupation` fields accurately reflect what you want to be recommended for (e.g., changing from "vlogger" to "podcast host, entrepreneur, angel investor").
* **Translate Wikidata into multiple languages [[09:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=540)]:** Localize your Wikidata entries so models reply accurately regardless of the language the user queries in.
* **Align all platform bios verbatim [[09:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=585)]:** When sources disagree, AI models tend to ignore you or pick safer competitors. Match your target keywords and descriptions word-for-word across Apple Podcasts, Spotify, Wikidata, and social channels.

### Step 4: Fix Technical Crawling & Indexing

* **Submit sitemaps via Google Search Console [[10:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=653)]:** Ensure every URL is registered in an XML sitemap submitted through Google Search Console so crawlers can systematically index every page.

### Step 5: Optimize Titles Based on Model Search Queries

* **Track internal search keywords [[12:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=731)]:** When models answer user prompts, they often perform internal background searches. Identify the exact query strings models generate and use them directly in episode titles and headlines (e.g., titling an episode to match `"The LinkedIn CEO Podcast"` [[12:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=746)]).
* **Use an AI search tracking tool [[11:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=702)]:** She recommends monitoring performance across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews using an AI search rank tracker (she highlights using **Peec AI** [[11:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=702)]).
* **Evaluate trends over weeks, not days [[16:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=972)]:** It can take 4+ weeks for crawlers and engines to update their indexes and reflect changes in chatbot answers.

### Step 6: Build Earned Media (PR & Third-Party Mentions)

* **Pursue external PR, listicles, and press [[14:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=878)]:** For competitive, broad queries (e.g., "Best AI Podcasts"), models rely on third-party aggregators and established editorial lists. She notes ~82% of what AI cites comes from earned media (Forbes, Reddit discussions, authoritative trade publications) rather than self-published claims [[16:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NFQ4-6MzuU&t=992)].

### Phase 1: Technical & Crawler Readability Audit

* **Run an AI visibility diagnostic:** Paste your website URL into an AI chatbot (like Claude or ChatGPT) and ask it how visible you are in AI search and what specific technical hurdles are preventing you from showing up.
* **Check for crawler-friendly HTML:** Ensure that your pages are pre-rendered or built with static HTML so that AI bots (from ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude) can read the source code instantly without waiting for heavy client-side JavaScript to render.
* **Publish full, plain-text transcripts:** Embed complete transcripts directly onto your content or episode pages (adding 8,000 to 15,000 words of crawlable text per episode) so models can easily read and quote your content with attribution.
* **Give every asset a dedicated URL:** Ensure every episode, article, or resource lives on its own clean, crawlable URL featuring summaries, transcripts, and profile details.

### Phase 2: Indexing & Infrastructure

* **Submit XML sitemaps to search engines:** Use Google Search Console (or direct lines to other engine indexers) to submit your site map, ensuring that all pages (such as newly discovered or unindexed pages) are systematically discovered and filed into search indexes.
* **Choose a reliable tech stack:** Host your site on platforms optimized for speed and dynamic content generation (the video highlights using tools like Claude Code for development, Vercel for hosting, and Railway to run data engines for dynamic page generation).

### Phase 3: Identity & Knowledge Graph Alignment

* **Update Wikidata entries:** Audit your structured records on Wikidata (which feeds Google Knowledge Panels and Gemini). Ensure entity fields like `instance of` and your professional occupation accurately match what you want to be recommended for (e.g., updating outdated titles like "vlogger" to "podcast host, entrepreneur, angel investor").
* **Localize structured data:** Translate your core entity identity and Wikidata entries into multiple languages so models can pull accurate descriptions regardless of the user's language.
* **Align platform bios word-for-word:** Harmonize your descriptions and taglines across every major reference point—including Apple Podcasts, Spotify, Wikidata, and social bios—using identical phrasing for your target keywords. When sources agree, AI models safely repeat your exact definitions.

### Phase 4: Keyword Matching & Tracking

* **Monitor performance with AI rank trackers:** Use an AI visibility tracking tool (such as Peec AI) to monitor whether your brand appears across major engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.
* **Identify internal model search queries:** Review insights from your rank tracker to see the exact phrases and query strings models use internally when answering prompts, then weave those exact phrases directly into your episode titles and page headlines.
* **Evaluate trends over weeks, not days:** Give search indexes and AI crawlers time to process updates—allow roughly 4 weeks for visibility metrics to start shifting.

### Phase 5: Building Earned Media & PR

* **Pursue third-party media coverage:** Because roughly 82% of what AI cites comes from earned media rather than self-published claims, focus on securing features in authoritative listicles, press mentions, external guest appearances, and active community discussions (like Reddit).
